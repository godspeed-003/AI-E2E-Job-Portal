PAPER REVISION GUIDE
How to rewrite "AI-BASED RESUME SCREENING AND AI-ASSISTED INTERVIEW EVALUATION
FRAMEWORK WITH PROCTORING SYSTEM" (rp-job.pdf, first draft) for IEEE submission
================================================================================

Written 2026-09-27 against rp-job.pdf and against the running system at commit
HEAD of feat/auth-video-interview-proctoring.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS
  A change list. Every item says what the draft currently claims, what the
  source code actually does, and what to write instead. Where a replacement
  sentence is given, it is given in full so it can be pasted.

WHAT IT IS NOT
  It is not a review of the writing quality. The draft's prose is serviceable.
  The problems are (a) claims the code does not support, (b) two reference
  lists, one of which is uncited and partly unverifiable, (c) a metrics section
  that is a plan rather than a result, and (d) three pages of a previous AI
  pass's notes left in the body.

HOW EVERY NUMBER IN HERE WAS OBTAINED
  From `metrics/run_all.py`, which imports the shipped modules and measures
  them. Reproduce the whole set with:

      .venv/Scripts/python -m metrics.run_all

  Output lands in `research paper/results/` (results.json, provenance.csv,
  tables/*.csv, unavailable.md) and `research paper/figures/`. Every quantity
  carries one of five labels and the label is load-bearing:

      measured        observed by running the shipped code
      derived         computed from measured values (ratios, deltas)
      specification   read out of the source; a constant, not a result
      simulated       produced against a constructed cohort or a fake provider
      unavailable     NOT MEASURED. Carries a `needs` field. Never has a number.

  If a number is not in results.json, it does not go in the paper.

1.3  SECTION III-C: "Raw resume text ... was parsed into structured fields ---
     including, at minimum, skills, prior roles, and years of experience ---
     prior to matching."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same defect as 1.2 and it is the draft's own [REQUIRED SPECIFICATION]. ANSWER:
there is no parsing stage. Rewrite III-C as a short subsection titled
"Stage 1: Text Extraction and Pre-filter" describing what does exist:

      1. `extract_text` --- PyMuPDF for PDF, direct read for text.
         Supported suffixes and an upload byte cap are enforced before parsing.
      2. `clean_text` --- whitespace and control-character normalisation.
      3. A length floor: `MIN_RESUME_WORDS = 40`. Below this the submission is
         rejected as unparseable rather than scored, which is the draft's
         "parsing failures were logged and excluded" behaviour and is honest as
         written.
      4. `ats_score(text, requirements)` --- the deterministic pre-filter:
         the percentage of the role's listed requirements that appear literally
         in the resume, by per-requirement regex, returned with the matched and
         missing lists. Integer 0-100. No model call.

      State the pre-filter's purpose explicitly, because it is a defensible
      design decision and the draft never makes the argument: the pre-filter is
      deliberately lexical so that an automated rejection can always be
      explained to the candidate as a named list of missing requirements. The
      threshold is `ats_reject_below = 40`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4  SECTION III-B: "S_resume = f_theta(R,J) in [0,1]" and
     "S_interview = g_phi(I,J,R) in [0,1]"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CODE: neither is in [0,1].
      - the pre-filter `ats_score` is an integer in [0,100]
      - the screening rubric is 5 criteria x 5 points = integer [0,25]
        (`CRITERIA = ("skill_match","experience","projects","communication",
        "culture_fit")`, `MAX_CRITERION = 5`, `MAX_LLM_SCORE = 25`)
      - the interview rubric is also 5 x 5 = integer [0,25]
        (`CRITERIA = ("technical_depth","problem_solving","communication",
        "culture_fit","practical_impact")`, `MAX_TOTAL_SCORE = 25`)
      - c(E) is an integer in [0,100]

WRITE: give the real ranges. Three different scales is not a flaw --- but the
      fusion step must then be shown to be scale-consistent, which it is
      (Algorithm 2 mixes only the two 0-25 rubric scores; c(E)/100 enters as a
      dimensionless weight). Say that in one sentence and a reviewer's
      objection disappears before it is raised.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5  SECTION III-B: "[REQUIRED SPECIFICATION: the exact functional form of h()
     ... e.g., a fixed weighted sum S_final = w1*S_resume + w2*S_interview with
     c(E) used only as a gating/flagging signal rather than a multiplicative
     term --- must be taken from the actual implementation and reported here
     verbatim]"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS NOW ANSWERED. It is neither of the two forms the draft guesses at.
`core/ranking.py`:

      t     = I / 100                                   (trust)
      v     = t * S_interview + (1 - t) * S_resume       (evidence)
      S_fin = (1 - lambda) * S_resume + lambda * v       (fusion)

with `DEFAULT_INTERVIEW_WEIGHT = 0.5` (lambda). So c(E) is neither a gate nor a
multiplier on the final score: it is the mixing weight *between* the two
evidence sources. Substituting gives the closed form

      S_fin = (1 - lambda)*S_resume + lambda*[ t*S_interview + (1-t)*S_resume ]

and the property worth a paragraph of its own: as t -> 0 the fused score
collapses onto S_resume, NOT onto zero. An untrusted interview cannot penalise
a candidate numerically; it can only stop the interview from helping them.
That is the right design for an integrity signal produced by a computer-vision
heuristic, and it is the difference between "integrity is advisory" and
"integrity is a rejection mechanism". Paste Algorithm 2 here
(`algorithms/alg2-fusion.tex`) and lift the prose from its comment block.

Also state the tiebreak, because ranking claims are meaningless without it:
ordering is by `(-S_fin, -S_resume, -ats, application_id)`. The final key is
not cosmetic --- 97 of 100 candidates in the audit cohort share an LLM score,
and before the id key was added the reported order moved 83 positions between
runs.

      SENTENCE-BERT IS NOT IMPLEMENTED AND CANNOT BE. `sentence_transformers`

      (a) DROP SBERT from the baseline list, or add the dependency and run it.

      (b) TF-IDF and BM25 are hand-implemented in `metrics/m4_baselines.py`,

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.7  SECTION III (integrity): the draft describes c(E) with a halving rule for
     repeated events, and carries a limitation paragraph about a scoring
     inversion.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CODE: the halving rule is gone. It was the cause of the inversion and it has
      been replaced. Current `proctoring/rules.py::compute_integrity_score`:

        for each distinct event kind k:
            s_k = worst severity weight observed for that kind
            d  += s_k * (1 + gamma * ln|E_k|)          gamma = 1.0
        d = d * min(kappa, 1 + sigma*(|K| - 1))        sigma = 0.25, kappa = 2.0
        integrity = clamp(round(100 - d), 0, 100)

      SEVERITY_WEIGHTS = {critical: 12.0, high: 6.0, medium: 3.0, low: 1.0}
      verdict: flag if < 55; clean if >= 80 and no critical event; else review

REWRITE III-F with the above and paste `algorithms/alg1-integrity.tex`.

THEN: the limitation paragraph must become a RESOLVED-DEFECT paragraph, and it
is one of the more publishable things in the paper because it is a specific,
reproducible, fixed error with a re-audit attached. The history:

      v1 (halving): six no-face events -> 79 (review), while a candidate with
          a phone, visible notes, a second voice and two off-screen glances
          -> 86 (clean). The more serious case scored HIGHER. The ordering was
          inverted.
      v2 (bounded saturation): scored a fully-absent candidate 88 (clean).
          Also wrong.
      v3 (shipped, above): monotonicity is now checked over 39 cumulative
          event sequences --- adding an event must never raise the score ---
          and that check passes. A single critical event scores 88; an event-
          free session scores 100.

Write it as: "An earlier revision of c(E) discounted repeated events by a
constant factor, which inverted the intended ordering between persistence and
severity: [the two cases]. The shipped rule replaces the discount with a
worst-severity-per-kind term plus a logarithmic persistence term and a capped
co-occurrence multiplier, and the property that motivated the fix ---
monotonicity under event accumulation --- is now asserted over 39 cumulative
sequences in the test suite."

"Precision@K, NDCG@K, MRR, Kendall's tau (RQ1); dR, R_rec, Correction Ratio

2.1  KEEP AND REPORT: NDCG@10, Kendall's tau-b, recovery rate, rank shift,
     injection resistance rate, boundary accuracy, offline-stage latency
     p50/p95, egress bytes.

2.2  DO NOT REPORT AS POINT ESTIMATES: Precision@10 and MRR.
     `metrics/m10_stability.py` re-draws the cohort under varying seeds and
     finds these two unstable across draws. results.json lists them by name in
     `quantities_too_unstable_to_report_from_one_draw`:
     precision_resume_only, precision_fused, mrr_resume_only, mrr_fused.

     The single-draw values look spectacular --- precision@10 0.50 -> 0.90,
     MRR 0.50 -> 1.00 --- and that is exactly why they must not be printed
     bare. Either report them with an across-seed interval or omit them. A
     jump to a perfect MRR from one draw of a synthetic cohort is the kind of
     number that makes a reviewer distrust every other number in the table.

2.3  DROP: peak RAM/VRAM and tokens/sec. Both require a local model run that
     has not happened. They are `unavailable` in results.json with a `needs`
     field. List them in Limitations as required future measurement, with the
     hardware spec that would need to be reported alongside them.

2.4  RESCOPE: "IRR" (injection resistance rate). Measured at 0.9032 over 31
     constructed attacks. The scope limit must be in the same sentence as the
     number, every time: this measures resistance to the five pattern families
     that were implemented, against a corpus the authors wrote. It is a
     regression test with a number attached. It is NOT a security evaluation
     and the paper must never say "secure against prompt injection".

2.5  ADD the honesty-label paragraph. This is a genuine methodological
     contribution and cheap to make:

     "Every quantity we report carries one of five provenance labels ---
     measured, derived, specification, simulated, or unavailable --- assigned
     by the measurement harness rather than by the authors at writing time.
     `unavailable` quantities are emitted with a machine-readable statement of
     what would be required to measure them and are never given a value. The
     complete labelled set --- 243 quantities, of which 131 are measured, 24
     derived, 37 simulated, 35 read from specification, and 16 declared
     unmeasured --- is released with the code."

     That paragraph is defensible, unusual, and it converts the paper's biggest
     weakness (many things unmeasured) into a stated method.

================================================================================
A CLOSING NOTE ON THE STRATEGY OF THIS REVISION
================================================================================

The draft's weakness is that it promises seven experiments and reports none.
The temptation is to fill the gap. Resist it: four of the seven cannot be run
without human raters, labelled media, or demographic data, and a fabricated
substitute for any of them is worse than the gap.

The stronger move is to make the gap itself part of the method. The harness
emits 243 labelled quantities, 16 of them explicitly `unavailable` with a
machine-readable statement of what each one needs. A paper that reports 227
quantities and names the 16 it cannot measure is more trustworthy than one that
reports 243 and hopes nobody checks. That discipline --- provenance labels
assigned by the harness rather than by the author at writing time --- is the most
novel thing in this work, and it costs nothing to claim because it is already
built.

Two findings are worth more than the pipeline itself, and neither is currently
in the abstract:

  - No resume-only method keeps over-sellers out of the shortlist. Four
    methods, 6-7 of 10 top slots occupied by candidates whose resumes overstate
    their concealed competence. That is a result about the whole class of
    resume-only screening, not about this system.

  - Interview answers are six times more exposed than resumes: 12 of 14 model
    calls carry them, against 2 for the resume. Every privacy analysis of AI
    hiring that focuses on the resume is looking at the wrong surface.

Lead with those.
