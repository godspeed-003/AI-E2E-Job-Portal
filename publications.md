## 1. The short answer

**Target workshops, and target the ACL family's workshops specifically.** That is
the revised recommendation, and this section explains why the "archival" column
below is the only column that matters when you pick one.

You have two publishable objects, not one.

**Paper A — the measurement.** *Does an adaptive AI interview correct the ranking
errors that static resume screening makes?* This is the research contribution. It
is a benchmark and an audit, and it produces numbers. 8 pages at a main
conference — but **4 to 8 pages at a workshop, reviewed by people who chose to
care about your exact topic.**

**Paper B — the system.** *Here is a working end-to-end hiring pipeline, and here
is what it refuses to do.* This is a demo paper. 2–6 pages plus a video. It is
easier to get accepted, and it does not compete with Paper A as long as the two
share very little text.

### The one thing to understand about workshops

You are right that workshops are easier to get into — typical workshop acceptance
rates run far above a main conference's. But **"workshop paper" does not mean one
thing, and the difference is whether you end up with a citable publication or a
conversation.** Verified today, from the venues' own pages:

| Workshop family | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| **ACL family** (EACL / COLING / NAACL / ACL / EMNLP) | **A real archival publication.** Proceedings go into the ACL Anthology with a DOI and an ISBN, published by the ACL. Verified against a concrete volume: `https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2025.realm-1/` — *Proceedings of the 1st Workshop for Research on Agent Language Models*, ACL 2025, DOI `10.18653/v1/2025.realm-1`, ISBN 979-8-89176-264-0, 34 papers. |

| **ACM RecSys** | Publisher not verified — see §2.9. Check before you rely on it. |

So: **an ACL-family workshop paper is the easy route that still counts.** It is
the recommendation.

### If you only do one thing

**Submit Paper A directly to an ACL-family 2027 workshop.** The earliest is the
EACL 2027 round, suggested direct submission **December 15, 2026 — 92 days from
today.**

Three reasons this beats the ARR-to-main-conference plan the previous version of
this document recommended:

1. **92 days instead of 28.** §6.9 costs the benchmark at 16 working days. Against
   the October 12 ARR deadline that leaves no room for the re-run you have not
   discovered you need. Against December 15 it is comfortable, and a comfortable
   experiment is a better experiment.
2. **It is still an ACL Anthology paper.** DOI, ISBN, indexed, citable, published
   by the ACL. Verified above against a real volume.
3. **Workshop reviewers are self-selected for your topic.** A workshop on
   evaluation, or agents, or NLP for social good, is staffed by people who
   volunteered because they care about that. That is a materially kinder review
   pool than a main track's.

**The catch, and it is the reason for the calendar reminder in §8: you cannot name
your target workshop yet.** The 2027 ACL-family workshop list is announced to
organisers on **October 2, 2026** — 18 days from today. The same day, AAAI-27's
workshop calls go up. **October 2 is the single most important date in this
document.**

### If you can do two things

Add **AAAI-27 Demonstrations, this Friday**. It is not a workshop, and it stays on
this list anyway because it is the only archival route whose remaining work is
already done: two pages and a five-minute video of a system that runs today.
`DEMO.md` Part A *is the video script* — it is timed to eight minutes; cut it to
five. Highest ratio of publication credit to remaining work on the whole list, and
the call explicitly permits concurrent submission, so it blocks nothing.

### If you can do three things

Submit to ARR on **October 12** as well — but for the reviews, not the venue. §3
explains the mechanism that makes this safe: ACL-family workshops accept
**pre-reviewed ARR commitments** on a *later* deadline than their direct one (EACL
workshops: direct Dec 15, ARR commitment Dec 22). So one ARR submission can be
committed to a workshop if the main-conference commitment does not work out. You
cannot do both routes with the same paper at once — see §3 for the dual-submission
rule that forbids it — but you can decide which one to use *after* you have read
your reviews.

### Read this before you plan travel

Several routes **require in-person attendance and explicitly forbid virtual
presentation** (AAAI-27 Student Abstracts and AAAI-27 workshops say so in as many
words; AAAI Demonstrations requires at least one author to register and present in
person; CHI is in Pittsburgh). FAccT 2027 explicitly offers a virtual option.

The workshop route has a real geography problem worth facing now, because the
staggered ACL deadlines are also a staggered map: **EACL 2027 is Athens (Mar 9–14),
COLING 2027 is Macau (May 9–14), NAACL 2027 is San Francisco (Jun 1–5), ACL 2027 is
Kyoto (Aug 17–22).** The earliest deadline and the cheapest flight are not the same
choice. Check each workshop's own page for whether it permits remote presentation —
individual ACL workshops often do, but the joint call does not promise it.

Decide the travel question **now**, not in December. An acceptance you cannot
attend is worth much less than one you can.

**Source:** `https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-27/demonstration-call/`
(loaded 2026-09-14)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Deadline | **September 18, 2026, 11:59 PM AOE (UTC-12)** |
| Notification | November 6, 2026, with detailed reviews |
| Camera-ready | November 20, 2026 |
| Demo sessions | February 18–21, 2027 |
| Format | 2-page system paper **plus one page of references only**, AAAI two-column |
| Video | up to 5 minutes; slides may substitute but "videos are weighted more heavily" |
| Anonymity | single-blind *or* double-blind, your choice |
| Archival | yes — "a short paper included in the proceedings", copyright transfers to AAAI |
| Novelty | must be previously unpublished *work*; the system may have been shown elsewhere recently, but the ideas must not be "established in mainstream products or services" |
| Concurrent submission | **explicitly permitted and encouraged**, including alongside AAAI/IAAI/workshop submissions |
| Attendance | at least one author must register and present in person |
| Submission system | OpenReview — **create the account before the deadline**, it is required |

**Why this fits.** The call wants a working system with a video. You have a
working system and, as of today, a script for filming it. The concurrent-submission
permission is unusual and valuable: submitting here does not block Paper A.

**The 2-page paper, section by section.** Do not write an intro-heavy research
paper; demo reviewers want to know what it does and whether the demo will work.

1. **What it is** (1 short para) — end-to-end hiring pipeline: apply → screen →
   AI video interview → proctoring → recruiter review.
2. **What is novel** (1 para) — the interview is a *second, independent
   measurement* permitted to overturn the resume ranking, and every decision is
   shown to the candidate.
3. **Architecture** (1 figure + 1 para) — reuse the `prd.md` §4 diagram. Name the
   three invariants; they are the reason the claim is credible.
4. **What the demo shows** (bulleted, 5 items) — lift the "money moments" from
   `DEMO.md`: the readable ATS rejection, the resume-grounded question plan, a
   live adaptive turn, prompt injection stripped rather than obeyed, the integrity
   report worded as evidence.
5. **What it deliberately does not do** (short para) — no speaker identification,
   no fullscreen detection, no fairness claim, proctoring is advisory. **Keep
   this.** Demo reviewers have seen a hundred over-claiming systems; a paper that
   states its limits in the 2-page version is memorable.
6. **Availability** — repository link, licence, and the fact that it runs fully
   local via Ollama with no API key.

**The video, in five minutes.** Open with a 30–60 second overview (the call asks
for exactly this), then: 45 s readable rejection → 60 s question plan and one
adaptive turn → 45 s injection stripped → 45 s recruiter's three tabs → 30 s
break the provider and show the interview survive → 15 s close. Record it with the
seeded database, not a live model call. Do two takes.

**The risk to name out loud:** four days. If you cannot record a clean video by
Thursday night, do not submit a bad one. Nothing here is lost — the NAACL 2027
System Demonstrations track (Dec 4), CHI 2027's Interactive Demos (Jan 21) and most
ACL-family workshops all take a system or demo paper later, and the workshop route in
§2.9 does not depend on this at all.

### 2.2 ✅ ARR October 2026 cycle → NAACL 2027 or COLING 2027 — *no longer the primary target; now the optional hedge*

**Read §2.9 first.** An earlier version of this document made this route the
headline recommendation. It is demoted for one reason: **October 12 is 28 days away
and §6.9 costs the benchmark at 16 working days**, which leaves no slack for the
re-run you have not discovered you need. An ACL-family workshop gets you into the
same ACL Anthology with 92 days instead of 28.

Keep this route on the table anyway, because ARR reviews are the most useful
feedback available to you at any price, and because the commitment mechanism below
is what powers the §3 hedge. **Take it only if the benchmark is genuinely finished
by early October.**

**Sources:** `https://aclrollingreview.org/dates` and
`https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp` (both loaded 2026-09-14)

ACL Rolling Review is a review pool, not a venue. You submit once; reviews and a
meta-review come back; then you *commit* the reviewed paper to a participating
conference. This is exactly the right mechanism for a first paper, because a
rejection from the commitment stage still leaves you holding reviews you can act
on for the next cycle.

**The October 2026 cycle, verbatim from the schedule table:**

| Milestone | Date |
| --- | --- |
| Submission | **October 12, 2026** |
| Reviewer registration | October 15, 2026 |
| Reviews due | November 16, 2026 |
| Author response | November 24 – November 30, 2026 |
| Meta-reviews released | December 17, 2026 |
| Cycle end | December 23, 2026 |

**Venues this cycle commits to:** NAACL 2027 and COLING 2027, both with ARR
submission October 12, 2026 and commitment December 23, 2026.

ARR moved from 8-week to 10-week cycles starting with the May 2025 cycle, so
deadlines "are set individually for each cycle rather than fixed to a particular
day of the month" — never assume next cycle's date from this one's.

**Hard requirements, each of which is a desk rejection if missed:**

- **Long paper: 8 pages of content. Short paper: 4 pages.** Unlimited references.
- **A limitations section is mandatory.** "Papers without a limitations section
  will be desk rejected." It does not count against the page limit. Write it
  first, not last — §6 of this document is a draft of yours.
- **Two-way anonymised.** No names, no affiliations, no identity-revealing
  self-citation, anonymised supplementary material and repository links. **No
  Dropbox-style links that can track downloads.**
- **No dual submission.** ARR "will not consider any paper that is under review in
  a journal or another conference at the time of submission", and you may not
  resubmit work already under review at ARR.
- **Text re-use is capped at 10% of tokens.** This is the number that governs the
  Paper A / Paper B split — see §3.
- **Preprints are allowed**; there is no anonymity period, per the 2024 ACL policy.
  Anonymous submission is rewarded on borderline papers rather than required.
- **Score floors bite later:** a paper whose meta-review scored 1.5 cannot return
  the next cycle; one scoring 1 "can never be resubmitted." Do not burn a cycle on
  a half-run experiment.

**On AI writing assistance:** "Generally, generative AI tools do not qualify for
authorship." Declare use and its scope in the Responsible NLP Checklist, with
specifics in the Acknowledgements. Language polishing, proofreading and predictive
typing need no disclosure; idea generation and machine-generated low-novelty text
must be disclosed and verified. You are wholly accountable for correctness.
See §7 — you *will* have something to declare, and declaring it costs you nothing.

**One trap specific to this project.** ARR states that "submissions attempting to
manipulate their machine 'readers', e.g. via prompt injections, may be desk
rejected." Your paper contains prompt-injection strings as *data*. Typeset every
payload inside a figure or a verbatim block, introduce it as a quoted example, and
never leave an imperative instruction sitting in running prose where an automated
pass could read it as directed at the reviewer. Consider rendering the payload
table as an image. This is a five-minute precaution against losing the whole
submission.

**Also verified, and it corrects a common assumption:** the ARR call for papers
says *nothing* about excluding system-demonstration papers. The constraint that
actually matters is the dual-submission rule in both directions — see §3.

*(For the best-fitting **workshop**, see §2.10 — RecSys in HR is closer still, and

| Final notification | March 23, 2027 |
| Conference | June 21–24, 2027 |

All cutoffs "11:59 PM Anywhere On Earth on the given date."

- **14 pages** excluding references, "most papers are between 12 and 14 pages",
  +1 page for ethics and adverse-impact statements, +1 more if accepted.
- **Anonymised**, and non-compliant papers are "rejected without review."
- **Archival or non-archival is your choice at submission and cannot be changed
  later.** Archival → ACM Digital Library. Non-archival → abstract only, but you
  still present and still get the same review.
- The 2027 page is a **papers-only call** — no 2027 CRAFT, doctoral colloquium or
  demo track is listed. Do not plan around a track that is not in the call.
- In-person expected, **virtual option available**.

**Why it fits.** Algorithmic hiring, transparency to the candidate, and Type I /
Type II error in screening are FAccT's home territory. This is the venue whose
reviewers care most about the exact thing your system does.

**Two reasons to be careful.**

1. **Every author must sign up to review**, and failure to do so "can lead to
   (desk) rejections." Reviewers and ACs are expected to have "prior experience
   reviewing or Area Chairing." For an undergraduate team this is a genuine
   obstacle — read the Author Guide and work out whether one author can carry the
   requirement before you commit to this venue.
2. **FAccT reviewers will ask about fairness, and `prd.md` says you do not do
   fairness auditing.** Do not paper over this. The honest position, which is a
   legitimate FAccT contribution: *we measure screening error and its correction,
   we do not disaggregate error by protected attribute because our synthetic
   generator does not encode protected attributes, and extending it to do so is
   the obvious next study.* A clearly scoped negative is publishable at FAccT. A
   vague gesture at "reducing bias" is not.

Fourteen pages is roughly double Paper A. Treat FAccT as the *extended* version of
the ARR paper, written in November once the experiments already exist — not as a
second experiment.

- That document quotes a 2026 workshop-guide rule that submissions "must be

Two lessons. First, **AI-usage policy changes year to year and page to page** —
read the policy for the exact venue and year you are submitting to, every time.
Second, the safe behaviour under both the 2026 wording and the 2027 wording is the
same: do the thinking yourself, disclose the assistance, and be able to defend
every number. See §7.

## 3. Dual submission: the rules, and the one hedge they allow

Five separate constraints, from five different pages, all verified today:

1. **ARR:** no paper under review elsewhere may be in ARR, and text re-use is
   **capped at 10% of tokens**.
2. **ACL-family workshops:** "Workshop organizers cannot accept submissions for
   publication that will be (or have been) published elsewhere."
3. **NAACL 2027 Demo:** while under review there, a paper may not be submitted to
   any other refereed venue, "as well as the ARR."
4. **NAACL 2027 Demo, within-track:** several papers from the same authors must not
   overlap in content or results by **more than 25%**.
5. **AAAI-27 Demonstrations:** concurrent submission **explicitly permitted**,
   including alongside AAAI and workshop submissions. The exception, not the rule.

### The rule you must not break

**One paper is in one review process at one time.** Concretely, the mistake to avoid:
you submit Paper A to ARR on October 12, then submit the same paper directly to an
EACL 2027 workshop on December 15 while the ARR reviews are still open. That
violates constraint 1 *and* constraint 2, and it is the kind of error that gets a
paper withdrawn rather than rejected.

### The hedge the rules do allow

Because ACL-family workshops run a **later ARR commitment deadline** than their
direct submission deadline (§2.9 — EACL workshops: direct Dec 15, commitment
Dec 22), one ARR submission gives you an option you exercise *after* reading your
reviews:

```text
Oct 12   submit Paper A to ARR
Nov 16   reviews arrive
Nov 24–30 author response
Dec 17   meta-review arrives  ─── now you decide, informed ───
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                     ▼
Dec 23   commit to NAACL/COLING main                  Dec 22   commit to an
         (harder bar, bigger prize)                            EACL 2027 workshop
                                                               (ACL Anthology,
                                                                easier bar)
```

**The two commitment deadlines are one day apart, so this is a real fork, not a
sequence.** You pick one. Note the ordering risk: the workshop commitment (Dec 22)
falls *before* the ARR cycle end (Dec 23), so decide on the 17th when the
meta-review lands, not on the 22nd.

**Two caveats.** Whether a given workshop accepts ARR commitments at all is
workshop-specific — proposals had to declare it, and it will be on the workshop's
page (§2.9). And this hedge only exists if you make the October 12 deadline; if the
benchmark is not ready, skip ARR and submit directly to a workshop on December 15
instead. **That is the simpler plan and it is not the worse plan.**

### Paper A and Paper B must be genuinely different papers

Not two crops of one manuscript. Make them different by *contribution*, not by
rewording:

| | Paper A (workshop, or ARR → main) | Paper B (AAAI Demo, then NAACL Demo) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim | static resume screening makes measurable Type I and Type II errors; an adaptive interview stage recovers a measurable fraction of them | a working, fully-local, transparent end-to-end hiring pipeline exists and here is what it refuses to do |
| Content | benchmark construction, baselines, ablations, metrics, results, threats to validity | architecture, invariants, the demo walkthrough, stated limits, availability |
| Evidence | numbers | the system running |
| Overlap risk | the architecture description — **keep it to a figure and three sentences in Paper A**, and cite the demo paper only after it is public |

**Concretely:** write Paper A's system description from scratch and short. Do not
paste Paper B's architecture section into it. That single discipline is what keeps
you under 10%.

**And if you are unsure, ask.** Workshop organisers, demo chairs and ARR support all
answer email — and workshop organisers are usually the most approachable people in
this document, because a workshop wants submissions. A one-paragraph question in
November costs nothing; a desk rejection costs a cycle.

## 6. Paper A: the research design

This is what makes the difference between "a student built a Streamlit app" and a
paper. The system is not the contribution. **The measurement is the contribution.**

### 6.1 The claim

> Static resume screening ranks candidates on a document, not on ability. Fluent
> self-presentation and genuine competence are different variables, so a
> document-only ranker makes two kinds of error: it promotes fluent over-sellers
> (Type I) and it buries capable people who write badly (Type II). We construct a
> benchmark in which both error types are injected by design, and we measure how
> much of that error an adaptive, resume-grounded AI interview stage recovers.

Note what is *not* claimed: nothing about predicting job performance, nothing about
fairness across protected groups, nothing about replacing recruiters. See §6.7.

### 6.2 The two cases

**Case A — the Over-Seller.** A synthetic candidate whose ground-truth competence
vector is low but whose resume is inflated: every requirement keyword present,
strong action verbs, quantified claims that the competence vector does not support.
A keyword or embedding ranker should rank them high. An interview that probes for
specifics should not.

**Case B — the Hidden Gem.** High ground-truth competence, badly written resume:
terse, missing keywords, no metrics, poor formatting, relevant work described in
plain language rather than in the job description's vocabulary. A keyword ranker
should bury them. An interview should find them.

Plus two controls, without which the two cases prove nothing:

**Case C — Honest Match** (competence high, resume accurate) and **Case D — Honest
Mismatch** (competence low, resume accurate). These measure your *false correction
rate*: a pipeline that shuffles everybody is not correcting anything, and the
controls are what distinguish correction from noise. **A reviewer will look for
these first.** Include them.

### 6.3 The pipeline under test

```text
Synthetic Resume Generator
        │   ground-truth competence vector c ∈ [0,5]^R over the role's R requirements
        │   resume text generated FROM c, then distorted per case
        ▼
Stage 1 — Resume screening (ATS keyword score → LLM evaluation, 5 criteria)
        │   → ranking_1
        ▼
Stage 2 — Adaptive interview  ⇄  Automated Candidate Response Agent
        │   agent answers FROM c, with case-appropriate verbosity and bluffing
        │   → transcript, turn-level scores
        ▼
Stage 3 — Final re-ranking (screening + interview + structured pros/cons)
        │   → ranking_3
        ▼
Metrics against c
```

Two roles minimum, one technical and one not: `amazon_backend_dev` and
`deloitte_marketing`. A finding that only holds for software resumes is a finding
about software resumes.

Target **≈50 multi-turn dialogues per case per role**. Report the exact n. Report
bootstrap confidence intervals, not bare means — with n=50 a difference of two rank
positions may be nothing.

### 6.4 Metrics — define these before you run anything

Fix the sign conventions in writing now, or you will misreport a result later.

| Metric | Definition | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ΔR** — Rank Shift Delta | `rank₃ − rank₁`, where rank 1 = best. Averaged per case. | Case A should be **positive** (demoted). Case B **negative** (promoted). |
| **R_rec** — Recovery Rate | fraction of Case B candidates below the Stage-1 shortlist cutoff that are above it after Stage 3 | higher is better |
| **FCR** — False Correction Rate | fraction of Case C/D controls whose shortlist decision flips | **lower is better**; this is the honesty check on R_rec |
| **CR** — Correction Ratio | corrected injected errors ÷ total injected errors, over Cases A and B | the headline number |
| **NDCG@k** | Stage-3 ranking against ground truth `c` | comparable to the baselines |
| **Kendall's τ** | rank correlation between final ranking and `c` | ordering quality |
| **Precision@k** | for the lexical baseline | standard IR |
| **MRR** | for the embedding baseline | standard IR |
| **IRR** — Injection Resistance Rate | fraction of injection attempts that neither shifted the score beyond tolerance ε nor caused an unjust rejection | *(named IRR, not ARR, so it never collides with ACL Rolling Review in your own text)* |
| **Stability** | score spread over N repeats of an identical input against the live provider | a measurement, **not** a pass/fail |
| **Cost / latency** | USD per candidate, seconds per turn, tokens per interview | the deployability argument |

### 6.5 Baselines — a paper without these will be rejected

| ID | System | Primary metric |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | BM25 / TF-IDF lexical ranker over resume vs job description | Precision@k |
| B2 | Sentence-BERT embedding similarity | MRR |
| B3 | Zero-shot LLM ranker: resume + JD in one call, rank out | Kendall's τ |
| B4 | **This pipeline**, full | NDCG, τ, CR |

B1 is the stand-in for a real ATS. B3 is the stand-in for "just ask GPT". If you
do not beat B3 you still have a paper — *"the expensive multi-stage pipeline does
not beat one prompt"* is a genuinely useful negative result and it is honest. Say
so if it happens.

| ID | Configuration | Isolates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A0 | full pipeline | — |
| A1 | Stage 1 only, no interview | the entire contribution of interviewing |

### 6.8 Threats to validity — write this section first

This is your mandatory ARR limitations section. Every item is a real weakness, and
naming it yourself is what buys the reviewer's trust.

1. **Ground truth is assigned, not observed.** The competence vector `c` is
   injected by construction, so we measure whether the pipeline recovers *the
   construct we planted*, not whether it predicts job performance. This is a
   construct-validity study. **Say this in the abstract**, not just the
   limitations — a reviewer who discovers it on page 6 stops reading.
2. **Self-preference bias.** The interviewer, the candidate response agent and the
   judge must not be the same model, or you are measuring a model's affinity for
   its own outputs. **Use a different provider for the candidate agent than for the
   interviewer and scorer, and report the pairing in a table.** The `llm/` provider
   factory already makes this a configuration change.
3. **LLM-as-judge.** Sample ~50 turns stratified across cases, have two humans
   score them against the same rubric, and report Cohen's κ against the model. This
   is a few hours of work and it is frequently the difference between "interesting"
   and "unconvincing".
4. **Synthetic resumes cut both ways.** They are an ethical strength — no real
   candidate's data, no consent problem, no PII — and a realism weakness. Release
   the generator and its prompts so the distribution is inspectable.
5. **Small n.** Report bootstrap CIs. Do not report a mean difference as a finding
   without one.
6. **Two roles is not "roles in general."** Say "on two roles" every time you state
   a result.
7. **No fairness claim.** `prd.md` lists bias/fairness auditing under *deliberately
   not built*, for the stated reason that doing it badly is worse than not claiming
   it. The generator does not encode protected attributes; error rates are
   therefore not disaggregated. State this plainly and name it as future work.
8. **Single-turn transcription is not evaluated.** Scores are computed from Whisper
   transcripts in the real system but from generated text in the benchmark, so the
   benchmark does not measure ASR-induced error. Name the gap.

### 6.9 Sixteen working days, honestly costed

| Days | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Synthetic resume generator: competence vector → resume, four case distortions. Seed it, fix the seed, commit the seed. |
| 2 | Candidate Response Agent: answers from `c`, per-case verbosity and bluffing, **on a different provider** |
| 2 | Harness: run N candidates × 4 cases × 2 roles through Stages 1–3, persist every artefact to a run directory under `data/tmp/` |
| 1 | Metrics module: every metric in §6.4, unit-tested against hand-computed toy inputs |
| 1 | Baselines B1–B3 |
| 2 | Run everything. Expect one full re-run after you find a bug in the runner. Budget for it. |
| 1 | Human-agreement spot check (§6.8 item 3) |
| 4 | Write 8 pages |
| 1 | Anonymise, checklist, limitations, references, injection-payload typesetting precaution |

That is 16 working days.

**Against the recommended workshop deadline (Dec 15) you have 92 calendar days.**
That is the single biggest practical argument for the workshop route: the same
16 days of work, done without panic. Spend the surplus on the three things that get
cut first under time pressure and that reviewers notice most — **the controls
(Cases C and D), the human-agreement check (§6.8 item 3), and the second role.** A
paper with all three is a different paper from one without them.

**Against the ARR deadline (Oct 12) you have 28 calendar days**, and the slack is
not spare — it is the re-run you have not discovered you need yet. That is survivable
only if you start this week and nothing surprises you. It usually does.

One thing to be careful about with the extra time: **a workshop page limit is
typically 4 or 8 pages, not 14.** More time does not mean a longer paper. Confirm
your workshop's limit on 3 October and write to it from the first draft — cutting 14
pages to 8 in December is a miserable way to spend the week before a deadline.

## 7. Ethics and AI-assistance disclosure

**Human subjects.** There are none. Every candidate in the benchmark is
synthetic — no real resumes, no consent, no PII, no IRB question. This is worth one
explicit sentence in the paper, because reviewers of hiring papers are primed to
ask.

**The system's own ethics.** State the three positions the build already takes,
because they are defensible and unusual:
- proctoring produces advisory evidence for a human reviewer and the interface
  never claims cheating;
- prompt injection in a resume is stripped and flagged rather than punished,
  because template resumes off the internet carry those strings and the candidate
  does not know;
- the candidate sees the keyword rule, the five criteria and the reasoning applied
  to them.

**AI assistance — declare it.** This project used AI assistance, including in
preparing this document. Every venue in §2 has a policy; all three policies I read
today reduce to the same behaviour:

- **ARR:** "Generally, generative AI tools do not qualify for authorship." Declare
  use and scope in the Responsible NLP Checklist, specifics in Acknowledgements.
  Polishing and proofreading need no disclosure; idea generation and generated
  low-novelty text must be disclosed and verified.
- **ICLR 2027:** mandatory disclosure section, not counted against the page limit;
  a model-generated falsehood or plagiarism is a Code of Ethics violation *by the
  authors*.
- **`reference_publications.pdf`** records stricter 2026 wording — submissions
  "must be primarily human-authored, reflecting original thought and analysis" —
  from `https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/WorkshopGuide`.

Write one honest paragraph, reuse it everywhere, and adjust for the venue's format:

> We used AI coding and writing assistants during development and manuscript
> preparation: for implementation, for drafting and editing prose, and for
> literature search. All experimental design decisions, all metric definitions and
> all reported results are the authors' own; every number in this paper was
> produced by the released code and verified by the authors. No text was included
> that the authors had not read and confirmed.

Then make the last sentence true. Verify every citation you keep — a hallucinated
reference is the single most common way an AI-assisted paper is caught, and it is
also the easiest to prevent.

- [ ] The **submission system** and whether it needs an account created in advance

**Six more, for workshops specifically.** A workshop's page is usually a single
short call, so these take five minutes each and they are where the surprises live:

- [ ] **Which conference is it attached to, and when?** One ACL-family call covers

## 10. What a reviewer will reject this for

Written as a pre-mortem. Fix these before submission, not after.

1. **No baseline.** A system paper with no comparison is not a research paper. §6.5
   is not optional.
2. **Assigned ground truth presented as real validity.** If the abstract implies
   you predict job performance, a reviewer who reaches §6.3 will reject on
   over-claiming alone. Put the construct-validity caveat in the abstract.
3. **LLM-as-judge with no human agreement statistic.** Cheap to fix (§6.8 item 3),
   fatal if missing.
4. **No controls.** Without Cases C and D, "the ranking changed" is
   indistinguishable from "the ranking is noisy."
5. **n too small, no confidence intervals.** A two-position rank shift with n=50
   and no CI is not a result.
6. **No limitations section.** At ARR this is not a criticism, it is an automatic
   desk rejection.
7. **A system paper wearing a research paper's clothes.** If your contributions
   list is "we built X, we used Y, we integrated Z", you have written Paper B.
   Submit it as Paper B, where it will do well.
8. **Fairness claims you cannot support.** Do not use the word "bias" as a
   benefit. The project deliberately does not audit fairness; say so.
9. **Missing reproducibility.** Release the generator, the seeds, the prompts, the
   run directories and the metrics code. Anonymise the repository link for ARR.
10. **A hallucinated citation.** Check every reference against its real source. One
    is enough to sink the paper's credibility and, with the AI-disclosure policies
    in §7, the authors' as well.

## 11. If it gets rejected

Which it may, and that is a normal outcome rather than a verdict on the work.

**The workshop route is built so that one rejection costs you seven weeks, not a
year.** The 2027 ACL cycle runs four sequential deadlines (§2.9), and a rejection at
one arrives in time for the next:

```text
Dec 15  EACL workshop ──reject (notified Jan 5)──┐
                                                  ▼
Jan  5  COLING workshop ──reject (notified Feb 6)─┐   ← same day as the EACL answer,
                                                  ▼      so prepare it in advance
Feb  5  NAACL workshop ──reject (notified Mar 26)─┐
                                                  ▼
Apr 25  ACL workshop
```

**Act on the reviews between rungs.** Four submissions of an unchanged paper is
four rejections. One rejection tells you what to fix; the seven weeks to the next
deadline is how you fix it. That loop — reject, revise, resubmit, with real reviewer
text in hand — is the actual point of this structure, and it is worth more to you
than an acceptance on the first try would be.

**If you went the ARR route, it is built for this too.** You keep the reviews and
the meta-review. Read them, fix what they identify, and resubmit next cycle — unless
the meta-review scored 1.5 (barred for one cycle) or 1 (barred permanently), which is
the real reason not to submit a half-run experiment.

**The ladder down, in order:** ACL-family workshop (four tries) → RecSys in HR 2027
if the 7th edition happens → AAAI or ICLR workshop for the presentation → CHI Student
Research Competition or Posters → arXiv preprint plus a strong repository. Note that
ARR permits preprints with no anonymity period, so an arXiv posting does not close
any door in the ACL ecosystem.

**And the thing that is true regardless of any venue's decision:** a working,
fully-local, transparent, honestly-documented hiring pipeline with 279 offline
tests and a benchmark that measures its own failure modes is a good final-year
project. The publication is a bonus on top of that, not a verdict on it.
